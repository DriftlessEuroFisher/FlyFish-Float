TrailWaterMaps works without an account, and most of what you do in it never leaves your phone. This page says what does, where it goes, and how to delete it.

  • On your phone only: your Field Book (favorites, notes, trips, floats, lake tracks) and your location.
  • If you make an account: your email address and the display name you choose, stored with Supabase, the service that runs our sign-in and database.
  • If you send an issue report: what you wrote, and your email only if you add it.
  • Live data (river flows, weather, map tiles) comes straight from public services to your phone.
  • No ads. Page views are counted with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies.

What stays on your phone

The Field Book: favorites, notes, the trips you log (with the flow and forecast saved at the time), floats, and the GPS tracks of lake trips. It is kept in this phone's storage for the app. We can't see it, and signing in doesn't change that: it is not copied to your account. There is no export yet, so the phone holds the only copy.

Your location is used on the phone to show the blue dot and, during a lake trip, to record the track you save in the Field Book. It is not sent to us.

The app also keeps settings, map areas you saved for offline use, and recent readings, so it opens fast and works with weak signal.

If a later version offers to back up your Field Book to your account, it will ask you first, and this page will say so before that happens.

Accounts (optional)

Signing in is only needed for features still to come, like sharing access points. You sign in with a 6-digit code sent to your email; there is no password.

  • What we store: your email address, the display name you choose (if any), and when the account was made. Supabase, which runs our sign-in, also keeps technical records of each sign-in, such as the time, the IP address and the browser type, as part of running the service.
  • Sign-in emails are sent by our email delivery service, Resend.
  • Who sees it: your email address is never shown to other people. Your display name isn't shown to anyone yet; once sharing features arrive, it will appear beside what you share.
  • What it's for: signing you in, and those features. Not marketing. We don't sell or share it.
  • To keep you signed in, the app saves a sign-in token on your phone until you sign out.

Issue reports

The Report an issue form sends the river or lake you named, what you wrote, the app's version number, and your email address only if you add it (so we can reply). Nothing else: not your location, and not your account. Reports are stored in our Supabase database and read only by the app's administrators.

Services your phone talks to directly

To show live conditions and maps, the app asks these services for data straight from your phone:

  • River flows and water temperature: the U.S. Geological Survey, and for some Colorado rivers the Colorado Division of Water Resources.
  • Weather: the National Weather Service, for a point on the river or lake you open.
  • Maps: map tiles from the USGS National Map (or OpenTopoMap, Esri or Google if you choose those basemaps); river lines from USGS hydrography and OpenStreetMap; public land from the USGS Protected Areas Database, hosted by Esri.
  • Fonts and the map library: Google Fonts and cdnjs (Cloudflare).

Each of these sees an ordinary web request from your phone, including its IP address and the area or river being asked about, and handles it under its own privacy policy. Map tiles are fetched for the area on screen, so while the map follows your location the map service can tell roughly where you are. Links to Google search, Reddit or Google Maps directions open those sites under their own policies.

Analytics

We count page views with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies, collects no personal information and doesn't follow you to other sites. There are no ads and no other trackers. The admin page shows only totals: how many accounts and how many reports.

Deleting your data

  • Your account: tap the person button, then Delete my account. Your account and profile are deleted from our server right away and can't be recovered. It doesn't touch the Field Book on your phone, and it doesn't delete issue reports you've already sent.
  • Your Field Book: it lives only on your phone. Deleting the app from your home screen, or clearing this site's data in your browser settings, removes it for good.
  • An issue report (for example, one with your email address in it): ask us to delete it, using the contact below.
